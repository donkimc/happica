Starting and stopping

Happica runs four local services: two copies of ComfyUI (one for images and video, one for music), the backend (the job queue), and this web app. One script starts them all:

cd ~/Project/happica
scripts/start.sh

Then open http://127.0.0.1:5173. Press Ctrl+C in that terminal to stop everything.

For story mode and narration, also open the Ollama app (it runs in the menu bar).

The status pill in the top-right corner tells you whether everything is ready:
  • Ready · mps — everything is running, on your Mac's GPU.
  • Ready · mps · story mode off — Ollama isn't running; line mode still works.
  • ComfyUI offline — the model server is still starting (give it ~20 seconds) or has stopped.
  • Backend offline — scripts/start.sh isn't running.

Using Happica from anywhere

The studio is also online at your Railway address, protected by a password. The website itself runs on Railway, but all the work still happens on this Mac through a secure Cloudflare tunnel — so the Mac must be on and scripts/start.sh running. While it runs, the Mac won't fall asleep on its own.

  • Password: set in the .env file in the project folder (HAPPICA_PASSWORD=…). After changing it, restart scripts/start.sh. Logins last 30 days per browser; Log out is in the ☰ menu (top right).
  • After a restart the tunnel gets a new address, which start.sh sends to Railway automatically — the website works again within about a minute.
  • Too many wrong passwords pause logins for 15 minutes.

Your first video

  1. Click the Narrated story preset at the top of the form.
  2. Type a short story — three or four sentences is plenty. For example: “A curious fox cub leaves its den on the first snowy morning. It chases snowflakes through a birch forest. At night it follows the northern lights home.”
  3. Pick a voice if you like, and check the time estimate next to Generate.
  4. Click Generate. The job appears below the form with a live progress bar showing each stage: scenes → narration → images → video → assemble.
  5. When it says Done, the video plays right in the page. Right-click it (or use the player's menu) to download.

You can keep using the app while a job runs. Jobs run one at a time — the GPU only fits one — so new jobs wait in a queue.

Scene modes

One line per scene

Each line you type becomes one scene, and the line is used directly as the image description. Use this when you know exactly what each shot should show. If narration is on, the line is also what gets spoken.

Story — AI writes the scenes

Write in plain prose. A local language model (Ollama) splits it into scenes and writes three things for each: a detailed image prompt, a motion hint for AI motion, and a line of narration. You'll see the narration in quotes under each scene once the job runs.

In both modes, tick Sound effects — the AI adds one to every scene to have a short effect (a door creak, birds, a magic chime) picked for each scene and mixed in.

My script — you write every scene

For stories written elsewhere (by you, or by Claude through the API). No story AI runs: each scene has

  • Picture — an English image description.
  • Narration — spoken exactly as written, in any voice language (Korean voices: st-ko-F1…).
  • Motion — the video's motion, or its own: pan & zoom, AI motion (LTX) or a still picture. One video can mix them, e.g. AI motion only for the two key scenes.
  • Sound effects — up to 4 per scene: what is heard, when (seconds after the scene appears) and how long (1–10 s). They play over the music without being ducked, and 6 dB lower while the narrator speaks.

Characters (name → look) keep cartoon characters consistent: wherever a picture names Ttori, it becomes Ttori (a tiny baby dokkaebi with sky-blue skin, one yellow horn…), so every scene describes them the same way. Paste a story file fills the whole form from JSON (title, style, voice, characters, scenes with image, narration, sfx).

Tips for better videos

  • Keep characters consistent. Each image is generated separately, so describe recurring characters the same way every time (“a red fox cub with a white chest and black paws”). If one scene drifts, use Regenerate with the corrected description.
  • Be concrete. “A wooden rowboat on a misty lake at dawn, soft golden light” beats “a peaceful morning”.
  • Short narration reads better. One or two sentences per scene, around 10–25 words.
  • Preview first. Try an idea with Quick preview or Narrated story, then switch to AI motion once the images look right — use Re-run, same seed to keep them.
  • Better storyboards: start with OLLAMA_MODEL=qwen2.5:7b scripts/start.sh. The larger model keeps characters more consistent than the default 3B one.

Images

Describe a picture, pick a style chip (photo, watercolor, anime, oil painting…), a size and how many to make (1–4). Each one is a different variation. More like this makes another set; Download saves the PNG. Pick a Character to put that person in the picture. Everything you make here can be turned into a video with Story Video → My pictures.

About 25 s to load the model, then ~5 s per image (~10 s with a character, ~15 s at 1024×1024).

Video from your pictures

In Story Video, choose My pictures (next to “One line per scene” and “Story”) to make the video from your own photos or from pictures in your Image library instead of generating new ones.

  1. + Add photos — choose several at once from your Mac or phone (JPEG, PNG, WebP and iPhone HEIC all work). Or + From Image library to pick pictures you made. Up to 30.
  2. Reorder with ↑ ↓ and remove what you don't want.
  3. Turn on Narration and choose who writes it:
    • I'll write it under each picture — type a line under each one; pictures without text stay silent.
    • AI writes it from the pictures — a local vision AI looks at every picture and writes one connected story in the voice's language (Korean voice → Korean narration). Add an optional line about what the video is about (“our family trip to Jeju last summer”) to steer it. Anything you type under a picture is used as written.
  4. Set the size, motion (pan & zoom, or AI motion — which uses the AI's description of each picture to animate it), voice, subtitles, music and ambience, then Generate.

Photos are cropped to fill the frame — keep the important part near the middle. Narrated pictures last as long as their narration; the others use “Seconds per scene”. Reading the pictures adds about 8 seconds per picture.

Music Studio

Open the Music tab at the top to make songs — with sung lyrics or instrumental — using ACE-Step 1.5, a local music model. Every song is made in two takes so you can pick the better one.

Simple — describe it

Describe the song in plain words, in any language: “an upbeat indie pop song about riding a bicycle through the city on the first warm day of spring”. Pick a length (and Instrumental if you want no vocals), then:

  • Write song — the local AI writes a title, style tags, lyrics sized to the length, and picks the BPM, key and language (about 15–25 s). It opens them in Custom mode so you can change anything before creating.
  • Write & create — same, but goes straight to making the two takes.

Songwriting uses qwen2.5:7b in Ollama (falls back to the story model if it isn't installed). The larger model keeps lyrics in your language and writes shorter, singable lines.

Custom — write it yourself

  1. Style: describe the genre, mood, instruments and voice — e.g. synthwave, energetic, warm synth pads, female vocals. Click the + chips to add common tags.
  2. Lyrics: write them in sections. Click [verse], [chorus], [bridge]… to insert a section tag where your cursor is. Or switch on Instrumental to skip lyrics.
  3. Pick the length (30 s – 4 min), BPM, key and time signature. For vocals, set the lyrics language (51 languages).
  4. Click Create song. The library card shows progress: writing the song (the model plans melody and structure), then rendering audio.
  5. Play each take with ▶, or click anywhere on the waveform to jump there. MP3 downloads the take.
Song lengthTime for 2 takes (M2, 16 GB)

2 min~5–6 min
  • Lyrics tips: short lines sing better than long ones. Repeat the chorus — songs need a hook. A 30-second song fits about one verse and one chorus.
  • Songs are mastered to streaming loudness (−14 LUFS), trimmed of trailing silence and faded out at the end.
  • Re-run makes two new takes with the same settings; Edit & re-run loads them into the form.

Music Video

Pick a song from your Music library (▶ previews it), describe the visual style, and choose roughly how many seconds per scene. The AI reads the song's lyrics and gives each scene its own lines to illustrate, in order — so the pictures follow the song. The cut matches the song's exact length, and the song plays at full volume. For instrumentals it tells a visual story that fits the mood.

A 30-second song with pan & zoom takes about 1.5 minutes. With AI motion, count ~6–7 minutes per scene.

Sound effects

The Sound FX tab makes effects and ambiences from a description — sea waves, a summer night, a Buddhist temple, falling leaves, rain on a window, a door creaking — using Stable Audio 3, a local model made for sound effects. Click a quick idea to fill in a description, or write your own.

Length3–45 seconds.
Seamless loopBlends the end into the start so the sound can repeat forever without a click or jump. Use it for ambiences (waves, rain, crickets); leave it off for one-shots (a door, footsteps, a whoosh). Loops repeat when you preview them.

Ambience in videos

In the Video tab, Ambience adds an environment bed under the whole video, alongside the music: Auto describes the setting from your story and makes a seamless loop (about 30 s extra), or pick any sound from your Sound library. Like the music, it dips while the narrator speaks. Finished videos can swap both with Change sound; New score / New ambience make a fresh Auto version.

Sung songs in story videos

In My script, Song puts a song from your Music library into the video (a song with Korean lyrics sings in Korean — make it in the Music tab with language ko). It can play three ways:

Sung intro & outroThe chorus is sung at full level over the opening and the ending, with no narration there; the song's own instrumental plays quietly under the narrated middle. Scenes without narration at the start and end of the script become the intro and outro (e.g. a title scene and a dancing finale); if there are none, the first scene waits for the chorus to end before its narration, and the last one stays on screen for the closing chorus.
Under the narrationThe whole song as background music, dipping while the narrator speaks.
Sung storyNo narration: the song tells the story, and each scene lasts while its share of the lyric lines is sung.

Sung lyrics appear as yellow subtitles, timed to the singing; narration subtitles stay white. To time them, Happica separates the voice from the music (Demucs) and listens to it with Whisper, then lines up the lyrics you wrote syllable by syllable — your words are shown, not Whisper's guesses. This takes about a minute the first time a song is used; after that it's stored with the song (and Music Videos show sung lyrics too).

TikTok 9:16 videos and captions

The TikTok story 9:16 preset makes pictures at 512×896 (the smallest portrait size the picture models handle well — smaller sizes break the pictures, they don't just blur them) and exports the finished video at 1080×1920. Captions are drawn after enlarging, so they stay sharp. Fast resize takes seconds; AI upscale (Real-ESRGAN) adds detail but takes about a second per frame — ~25 minutes for a 1-minute video.

Captions & export in My script sets the caption font — Rounded (Jua) or Handwritten (Gamja Flower), both friendly Korean faces, or the plain system font — the size, bottom or lower-third position, and an optional dark box behind the text. On portrait videos a narration line shows as one or two lines at a time, split at sentence ends, instead of many quick flashes.

Voiceover

Paste up to about 5,000 characters, pick one of 294 voices in 33 languages (grouped by language; Korean has 5 female and 5 male voices) and a speed. Blank lines between paragraphs become longer pauses. You get an MP3 to download and a matching .srt subtitle file. A minute of speech takes only a few seconds to make.

Characters

  1. Upload one clear face photo — eyes open, looking at the camera, good light, the face filling much of the picture (the face model copies what it sees; a photo with closed or hidden eyes gives odd, mismatched eyes) — give the character a name, choose what they are (woman, man, girl, boy, person), and describe their look — hair, eyes, skin, anything distinctive.
  2. Tick the consent box: use only photos of yourself, or of people who agreed to it.
  3. Create character makes a 4-picture character sheet (about 45 s).
  4. Under the character, type a change — or click an idea like as a 70-year-old, as a child, as a medieval knight — and Create.
  5. Use them elsewhere: pick them as the Character in Images, or as the Main character in Story Video. In story mode the AI writes them into the story; in line mode, write their name in the lines where they appear.

The face model keeps facial features; the look description keeps hair and details consistent when the character is aged or restyled — fill it in. Each picture with a character takes about 10 s more. Character pictures are made at 768×768 so eyes come out right (at 512 they are tiny and often mismatched). Use the Turbo picture model with real-person characters: with Lightning the face isn't kept.

3-view sheet (3면도)

Under any character, 3-view sheet (3면도) draws the character from the front, side and back (about 2 minutes) and shows them side by side; each view is also saved as its own picture. The front view is drawn first; the side and back views are drawn from it, and the local picture-reading AI writes the front view's clothes into their prompts, so the outfit stays the same in all three. For cartoon characters, Use these views as reference pictures makes scenes draw the character from all three angles. Limits: the "side" view often comes out three-quarter rather than a true profile, and cartoon characters' back views sometimes still face front — draw another sheet if one view is off.

Texture (인물 질감)

In Images and under each character, Texture changes how skin, hair and fabric look: Smooth redraws the last part of each picture for softer, cleaner skin (a few seconds more); Detailed sharpens the finished picture and adds a fine film grain, so skin and fabric read as more textured (instant). As drawn leaves the picture as the model made it.

Cartoon characters from reference art

The face model only works for real people. For picture-book characters — the 한복 kids, 아기도깨비 — choose Cartoon (reference art) in the Characters tab, upload 1–3 pictures of the character (a clear front view on a plain background works best), and give it a name and look. Happica draws a 4-picture sheet with IP-Adapter, which carries the character's colours, shape and outfit from your pictures into new ones. In My script, tick the character under Cartoon characters; every scene whose picture names it is drawn from its pictures, and its look is spelled out in the prompt too. If a scene names two cartoon characters, the first one named gets the reference.

Prompt helpers for AI chatbots

Want better results? Use an AI chatbot (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini…) to write your Happica input. Each helper below teaches the chatbot which model the tool uses, what it does well and badly, and which settings exist — and asks it to answer in the exact fields of the Happica form.

  1. Click Copy on the helper for the tool you want.
  2. Paste it into a new chatbot conversation, then type your idea right after it (or after the last line, “My request:”).
  3. Copy each field of the answer into the matching box in Happica.

The chatbot never sees Happica — it only writes text for you to paste. You can keep chatting (“make it darker”, “shorter lyrics”) and it keeps the rules.

Images

You are helping me write input for the Images tool of Happica, a local AI studio.

How the tool works:
- Model: SDXL-Turbo (Stable Diffusion XL, distilled to 4 steps). It runs with guidance turned off, so negative prompts and "no X" have NO effect — describe only what should be there.
- The prompt is read by a CLIP text encoder that only uses about the first 60 words. Put the most important things first.
- Works best with comma-separated descriptive phrases: subject, appearance, action, setting, lighting, camera/composition, mood.
- Weak at: readable text or logos in the image, exact counts of objects, hands and complex poses, two or more specific people interacting, precise left/right placement. Avoid relying on these.
- Strong at: single clear subjects, landscapes, interiors, food, products, portraits, and art styles.
- Fields in the form: Prompt; Style (added to the end of the prompt, e.g. "watercolor illustration", "photorealistic, 35mm photo", "anime style", "oil painting"); Size (768x512 landscape, 512x768 portrait, 512x512 square, 1024x1024 square = slower); Images (1-4 variations); Character (optional: a saved person — if I name one, write their name in the prompt where they appear).

Your job: turn my idea into the best input for this tool. Keep the prompt under 60 words. Do not use "no", "without" or "not". Pick the size that fits the composition.

Style: ...
Size: ...
Images: ...
Tip: one short sentence on what to change if the result isn't right.

Music

You are helping me write a song for the Music tool of Happica, a local AI studio.

How the tool works:
- Model: ACE-Step 1.5 (turbo) with a 1.7B "song planner". It makes full songs with sung lyrics, or instrumentals, 30 seconds to 4 minutes. Two takes per song.
- Style field: comma-separated tags describing only how the music SOUNDS: genre, sub-genre, mood, 3-5 instruments, production feel, and the vocal type (e.g. "female vocals", "male vocals", "duet", "choir"). 6-12 tags, lowercase. No artist or song names (they don't work and imitate real artists), no tempo or key in the tags (those have their own fields).
- Lyrics field: section tags on their own lines in square brackets: [intro] [verse] [pre-chorus] [chorus] [bridge] [outro]. Short singable lines (4-9 words), simple rhymes, a chorus with a repeated hook line. No stage directions, no notes, no chords. Leave a blank line between sections.
- How much fits: 30 s = [verse] + [chorus] (4 lines each). 60 s = verse, chorus, verse, chorus. 90-120 s = intro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, chorus, outro. Longer = add pre-choruses and a final double chorus. Too many lyrics for the length get rushed or cut.
- Other fields: Title; Instrumental (on = no lyrics); Length (30 s, 1, 1.5, 2, 3 or 4 min); BPM (30-300); Key (e.g. "A minor", "E major"); Time (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 6/4); Lyrics language (51 languages; the lyrics must be written in it).

Your job: write a complete song for my idea that fits the chosen length. Pick a BPM, key and time signature that suit the mood (minor keys for sad or dark songs).

You are helping me plan a music video in Happica's Music Video tool.

How the tool works:
- I pick a song I made in Happica's Music tab. A small local AI splits the song's lyrics into one slice per scene, in order, and writes a picture for each slice; pictures are made with SDXL-Turbo and cut to the song's exact length. For instrumentals it invents a visual story from the song's mood.
- What I control: Visual style (one line, added to every picture, e.g. "soft pastel watercolor, storybook illustration"), Size (768x512, 512x768, 512x512), Motion (pan & zoom, or AI motion with LTX-Video: subtle natural movement, about 6-7 minutes per scene), and Seconds per scene (2-6; the number of scenes = song length / this).

2) Recommend Size, Motion and Seconds per scene.
3) If the lyrics are too abstract to picture, suggest small lyric edits that keep the meaning but add concrete imagery (I can re-make the song with them).

Answer in exactly this format:
Visual style: ...
Size: ...
Motion: ...
Seconds per scene: ...
Lyric suggestions: (or "none")

Sound FX

You are helping me write descriptions for the Sound FX tool of Happica, a local AI studio.

How the tool works:
- Model: Stable Audio 3 small SFX, made for sound effects and ambiences (not music, not intelligible speech). Clips are 3 to 45 seconds.
- Best description: ONE sentence (20-40 words) of what is heard: the sound sources, their texture and rhythm (steady, intermittent, rising), how near or far they are, and the space they're in (small room, cave, open field, street). Example: "gentle ocean waves rolling onto a sandy beach, soft foam hiss, distant seagulls, calm open seaside ambience".
- Don't describe visuals, emotions or stories, and don't ask for music or words.
- Settings: Length (3, 5, 10, 20, 30, 45 s); Seamless loop (on for ambiences that play under a video — waves, rain, night, crowd; off for one-shots — a door, footsteps, a whoosh, an explosion); "AI adds detail" (a local AI expands short ideas; turn it off when my description is already detailed); Takes (1-2).
- One-shots should be short (3-5 s) and describe a single event from start to end. Ambiences should be 10-30 s and steady, with no big one-off events, so they loop well.

Your job: for each sound I ask for, write the best description and settings. If I ask for a whole scene ("a haunted house"), split it into separate sounds: one ambience bed plus a few one-shots.

Answer in exactly this format, one block per sound:
Sound: (short name)
Description: ...
Length: ...
Seamless loop: on/off
AI adds detail: off

Voiceover

You are helping me write a script for the Voiceover tool of Happica, a local AI studio.

How the tool works:
- Voice models: Kokoro and Supertonic 3, 294 voices in 33 languages: English (US/UK), Korean, Japanese, Mandarin, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Hindi, Arabic, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Turkish, Dutch, Polish, Swedish, Danish, Finnish, Greek, Czech, Hungarian, Romanian, Ukrainian, Bulgarian, Croatian, Slovak, Slovenian, Estonian, Latvian, Lithuanian. Korean and the other Supertonic languages have 10 voices each (Female 1-5, Male 1-5). The voice reads exactly the text it gets, so the text must be in the voice's language.
- It reads punctuation as rhythm: commas = short pause, periods = longer pause, a blank line between paragraphs = a clear pause. Question marks raise the tone.
- It cannot whisper, shout, laugh, change emotion on command or read stage directions — anything in brackets or asterisks is read aloud or ignored. Emotion comes only from the words and punctuation.
- Avoid: abbreviations (write "for example", not "e.g."), symbols (&, %, $ → words), emoji, URLs, markdown, all-caps, and long numbers (write them as spoken: "twenty twenty-six", "three point five million").
- Up to about 5,000 characters. Speed is adjustable (0.6x-1.5x); about 150 words = 1 minute at 1.0x.
- Output: an MP3 and a matching subtitle file.

Your job: write or rewrite my script so it sounds natural when spoken: short sentences (under 20 words), conversational, clear paragraph breaks where the topic changes. Keep my meaning and facts. Tell me the target length.

Answer in exactly this format:
Voice: (language, female/male, and the feel: warm, calm, energetic…)
Speed: ...
Estimated length: ... seconds
Script:
(the text, ready to paste)

REST API for other apps

Other programs — on this Mac or anywhere through your public address — can make videos, images, music, sound effects and voiceovers with an API key. Everything a person can do in the tabs, the API can do.

  1. Open the ☰ menu (top right) → API keys, give the key a name (e.g. “Batch video app”) and Create key. Copy it right away — it's shown only once. Revoke a key there anytime.
  2. Send it with every request: Authorization: Bearer hap_… (or X-API-Key: hap_…).
  3. Open the interactive API docs at /api/v1/docs (while logged in) to see every field and try calls in the browser.

Addresses

  • Apps on this Mac: http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/… (fastest)
  • Anywhere else: https://<your Railway address>/api/v1/… (the Mac must be running start.sh)

Endpoints

CallWhat it does
POST /videosA video. scene_mode: lines (one line per scene), llm (story — AI writes the scenes), slides (your pictures, upload first), musicvideo (with song_track), script (your own scenes: script = list of {image_prompt, narration, motion, motion_mode, sfx: [{prompt, at, duration, volume}]}, plus characters = name → look). sfx_auto: true adds AI-picked effects in lines/story modes. Same options as the Story Video form: narration, voice, subtitles, motion, music, ambience, character…
POST /images1–4 images (prompt, style, size, character)
POST /musicA song or instrumental — and POST /music/draft to let the AI write the lyrics first
POST /sfxA sound effect or seamless ambience loop
POST /voiceoversNarration MP3 + subtitles from text
POST /charactersA reusable character from an uploaded face photo
POST /combine (API only)Join finished videos — or finished music, sound effects and voiceovers — into one long file, e.g. a 1-hour ambience or music mix. items: [{"job": "…", "take": 1}]; target_seconds repeats them up to that length (max 4 hours); order: sequence or shuffle; fade_in/fade_out seconds; width/height for video (clips of other sizes are letterboxed). A 1-hour video takes seconds.
POST /upscale (API only)Make a finished video, image or character job (job) — or an upload (path) — bigger: target_height (720, 1080, 1440, 2160…) or scale (1.5–4). method: ai (Real-ESRGAN redraws detail — ~25 s per image, ~0.8 s per video frame) or fast (plain resize, seconds). Sound is kept.
POST /uploadsUpload a picture (multipart field file) for slides, characters or upscaling — or a video (MP4, MOV, WebM, MKV, up to 1 GB) for upscaling; returns its path
GET /jobs/{id}Status, progress and files (each with a role — video, audio, image, subtitles… — and a download url)
GET /jobs/{id}/wait?timeout=600Waits until the job is done (or the timeout passes) and returns it — the simplest way to run jobs one after another
GET /jobs?kind=video&state=doneList jobs (newest first)
POST /storiesA whole story in one call: title, style, characters (name → look), art_characters, voice, motion, scenes (image, narration, sfx as text or cues, motion_mode), song (a new song's title/tags/lyrics/bpm/key/language/duration, or track; placement defaults to sung intro & outro when it has lyrics), hashtags, caption. Makes the song, pictures, narration, sound effects, video and the 1080×1920 export as one job. Its files: final (the video), cover (first scene with the title) and caption (title + caption + hashtags).
POST /stories/batchUp to 20 stories, made one after another; song_reuse: true makes one song and uses it in every story. Returns all job ids.
song, caption_style, output_height, art_characters on POST /videosThe same building blocks for any video: a sung song (track or spec, placement bed / intro_outro / sung_story), caption font/size/colour/position/box, export size (upscale fast or ai), cartoon characters. POST /characters with kind: "art" and refs (1–3 uploads) makes a cartoon character.
POST /jobs/{id}/scenes/{i}/regenerate, /scenes/{i}/sfx/{k}/regenerateRedo one scene's picture (optional new prompt), or one of its sound effects (another take, or a new prompt); the video is rebuilt
POST /jobs/{id}/cancel, /rerun, DELETE /jobs/{id}Cancel, run again, delete
GET /voices, /music/options, /healthVoice ids, music keys/languages, engine status

Every generation call answers right away with a job (state: "queued"). Jobs run one at a time on the Mac, in order. Add "webhook_url": "http://…" to any generation call to get a POST with the finished job (event: job.done, job.failed or job.cancelled). To download a file, request its url with the same key header.

Example: make a narrated story video and download it

KEY=hap_your_key_here
JOB=$(curl -s -X POST http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/videos \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"prompt":"A fox cub explores a snowy forest and finds its way home.",
       "scene_mode":"llm","max_scenes":4,"narrate":true,"voice":"af_heart",
       "subtitles":"burn","motion":"kenburns","music":"auto"}' | jq -r .id)
URL=$(curl -s "http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1/jobs/$JOB/wait?timeout=1800" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $KEY" | jq -r '.files[] | select(.role=="video") | .url')
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $KEY" -o fox.mp4 "$URL"

Example: Python

import httpx

api = httpx.Client(base_url="http://127.0.0.1:8000/api/v1",
                   headers={"Authorization": "Bearer hap_your_key_here"}, timeout=1900)
job = api.post("/images", json={"prompt": "a lighthouse at night", "count": 2}).json()
job = api.get(f"/jobs/{job['id']}/wait", params={"timeout": 1800}).json()
for f in job["files"]:
    open(f["name"], "wb").write(api.get(f["url"]).content)

Example: a story file in one call

story = json.load(open("stories/test01_ttori.json"))   # title, style, characters, voice, scenes, song
story["hashtags"] = ["동화", "아기도깨비", "해삐한무지카"]
job = api.post("/stories", json=story).json()
job = api.get(f"/jobs/{job['id']}/wait", params={"timeout": 3600}).json()
files = {f["role"]: f for f in job["files"]}            # final, cover, caption, subtitles, scene_image…
open("final.mp4", "wb").write(api.get(files["final"]["url"]).content)

Example: a 1-hour 1080p video from short clips

clips = ["312157cf81d4", "0ba000c22d45"]          # finished video jobs (~30 s each)
big = []
for c in clips:                                    # 1) upscale each short clip (~10 min each)
    j = api.post("/upscale", json={"job": c, "target_height": 1080}).json()
    big.append(api.get(f"/jobs/{j['id']}/wait", params={"timeout": 3600}).json()["id"])
j = api.post("/combine", json={"items": [{"job": b} for b in big],   # 2) repeat to 1 hour (seconds)
                               "target_seconds": 3600, "order": "shuffle", "fade_out": 5}).json()
job = api.get(f"/jobs/{j['id']}/wait", params={"timeout": 3600}).json()

Upscale first, then combine: combining copies the clips instead of re-encoding, so upscaling a 1-hour file would take ~100× longer than upscaling its short clips.

Treat keys like passwords: anyone with one can use your Mac's GPU and download what you've made. Use one key per app so you can revoke them separately. Wrong keys are rate-limited like wrong passwords.

Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
Generate is greyed out in story modeOllama isn't running, or the model isn't installed. Open the Ollama app; if the message says the model is missing, run ollama pull qwen2.5:3b.
A job failedIts error appears in red on the job. Images made before the failure are kept. Re-run it, or check outputs/logs/comfyui.log and outputs/logs/backend.log.
“ComfyUI offline” stays onRestart with Ctrl+C and scripts/start.sh. Look at the end of outputs/logs/comfyui.log for the reason.
AI motion is much slower than ~7 min a sceneThe Mac is short on memory and swapping. Close other apps (browsers with many tabs, other AI tools) and try again.
Narration sounds wrong or garbledThe voice and the text are in different languages. Pick a voice that matches the story's language.
“The music engine isn't running”The music copy of ComfyUI (port 8189) is down. Restart with scripts/start.sh; its log is outputs/logs/comfyui-music.log.
A character changes between scenesSee “Keep characters consistent” above — Regenerate the odd scene with a precise description.
